Adding a roof window to your loft: what to know before you start

Thinking of adding a roof window or skylight to your loft? Planning rules, Building Regulations, sizing and positioning explained in plain English.

Skylights5 min readBy Sam Double Glazing

A roof window can turn a dark loft into a bright, usable room. Because it sits in the slope of the roof, it lets in far more daylight than a wall window of the same size. Here's what to think about before you go ahead.

Do you need planning permission?

In many cases, no. Roof windows on houses are often allowed under "permitted development", as long as they don't stick out too far from the roof slope and side-facing ones use obscure glass and are high enough or non-opening.

The rules are different for flats and maisonettes, listed buildings and homes in conservation areas, and some London streets have extra restrictions. If any of these apply, check with your local council before you order.

What about Building Regulations?

Cutting a new opening in the roof changes the structure, so the roof timbers around the window need to be supported properly. New windows also need to meet the energy-efficiency rules. If the loft is being converted into a bedroom, there are fire-escape requirements too — some roof windows are designed to double as an escape window.

We'll explain what applies to your roof when we quote.

Choosing the size and position

  • Height: place the window so you can see out comfortably when standing, and reach the handle easily.
  • Width: sizes are usually chosen to fit between the roof rafters, which avoids extra structural work.
  • Two smaller or one large? Two windows side by side often spread light more evenly than one big one.
  • Which side of the roof: south-facing windows give the most sunlight but can get warm in summer — blinds help.

Opening styles

  • Centre-pivot: the most common type, easy to clean from inside.
  • Top-hung: opens outward from the top, giving a clearer view out.
  • Fixed: for light only, in places you can't reach.

Replacing an old roof window

If an existing roof window leaks, has misted glass or is hard to open, it can often be replaced with a modern one of the same size, which keeps the work simple.

Bifold vs sliding vs French doors: which suits your London home?

Bifold, sliding or French doors? A plain-English comparison of light, space, security and cost to help you choose the right garden doors.

Doors5 min readBy Sam Double Glazing

If you're opening up a kitchen, living room or extension to the garden, the big question is which type of door to choose. Here's a straightforward comparison of the three most popular options.

Bifold doors

Bifolds are made of several narrow panels that fold back against each other like a concertina, so you can open almost the whole width of the opening.

  • Best for: wide openings and people who want an indoor–outdoor feel in summer.
  • Good to know: there are more frames across the glass, so the view is a little more broken up when the doors are closed. The folded panels also need space at the side.
  • Handy extra: most bifolds can have a "traffic door" — a single panel that opens on its own for everyday use.

Sliding patio doors

Sliding doors have large glass panels that glide past each other on tracks.

  • Best for: maximum light and the best view when closed, and rooms where there's no space for doors to swing.
  • Good to know: you can only open part of the width at once — usually half on a two-panel door.
  • Handy extra: modern aluminium sliders have slim frames and smooth running.

French doors

French doors are a pair of doors that open from the middle, usually outwards.

  • Best for: smaller openings, period homes and anyone who wants a classic look.
  • Good to know: the doors need clear space to swing open. Adding side panels makes a small opening feel bigger and lets in more light.
  • Handy extra: often the most affordable of the three.

Quick comparison

  • Most light when closed: sliding doors
  • Widest opening: bifold doors
  • Smallest openings: French doors
  • No swing space needed: sliding doors

uPVC or aluminium?

uPVC is warm, low maintenance and good value. Aluminium is stronger, so the frames can be slimmer and the glass bigger — which is why it's popular for bifolds and large sliders. Both come in a range of colours.

Security

All three types can be fitted with multi-point locking and toughened or laminated safety glass. We'll explain the locking on any door we quote for.

Misted double glazing: why it happens and how to fix it without a new window

Condensation between your window panes means the sealed unit has failed. Here's why it happens, why wiping won't help, and how it's fixed without replacing the whole window.

Repairs4 min readBy Sam Double Glazing

If your windows look cloudy or have droplets of water between the two panes of glass, you're not alone — it's one of the most common window problems we see across London. The good news is that it usually doesn't mean you need a new window.

Inside, outside or in between?

First, check where the condensation is:

  • On the inside of the glass (room side): this is normal condensation from cooking, showers, drying clothes and breathing. Better ventilation — opening trickle vents, using extractor fans — usually sorts it out.
  • On the outside of the glass: on cool mornings this can actually be a good sign. It means your glazing is keeping the heat inside, so the outer pane stays cold.
  • Between the panes: this means the sealed unit has failed. This is the one that needs fixing.

Why the seal fails

A double glazed window is built around a sealed glass unit: two panes of glass with a spacer bar around the edge and a sealed gap between them. The spacer contains a drying agent that keeps the gap free of moisture.

Over the years, sunshine, temperature changes and movement in the frame can weaken the seal. Once it fails, damp air gets in, the drying agent becomes saturated, and moisture condenses on the inside faces of the glass — exactly where you can't wipe it.

Common causes include age, a unit that was badly fitted or not packed properly, blocked drainage in the frame, and damage to the edge of the glass.

Why it's worth fixing

  • A failed unit doesn't insulate as well, so the room loses more heat.
  • Misting gets worse over time, and can leave permanent white staining on the glass.
  • It makes the window look tired, which matters if you're selling or letting.

The fix: a new sealed unit, not a new window

In most cases we don't need to touch the frame. We measure the glass, have a new sealed unit made to size, then remove the old unit and fit the new one into your existing frame, with new glazing packers and seals where needed.

It's usually much cheaper than replacing the whole window, and it's normally done in a single visit once the new unit is ready.

If the frame itself is damaged, rotten or badly draughty, we'll tell you honestly and quote for a replacement window instead.

What about "de-misting" services?

Some companies drill holes in the glass to dry out the gap. It can improve the look for a while, but it doesn't restore the seal, so we recommend a new sealed unit for a lasting fix.

Terms & conditions

The terms that apply to quotes and work carried out by Sam Double Glazing Ltd.

About us

Sam Double Glazing Ltd is registered in England and Wales, company number 16634728, Thornton Road, Ilford, IG1 2ER.

Quotes

Quotes are free and without obligation. A quote is based on the information and measurements available at the time and is confirmed after a survey. Designs created with our online configurator are a guide only; the final design is confirmed at your free survey.

Orders and payment

An order is confirmed when you accept our written quote and pay the deposit stated on it. The balance is due on completion of the work. Products are made to measure for your property.

Cancellation

If you entered the contract at your home or away from our premises, you may have a legal right to cancel within 14 days. Because products are made to measure, if you cancel after manufacture has started at your request we may charge for costs already incurred, as allowed by law.

Installation

We'll agree a date for installation. Please make sure we have clear access to the work areas. We'll remove the old windows or doors and leave the site tidy.

Guarantees

  • Frames — 10 years
  • Sealed glass units — 5 years
  • Locking mechanisms (locks, handles and hinges) — 2 years

Guarantees cover faults in materials and workmanship and don't cover accidental damage, misuse or normal wear. They are in addition to your statutory rights.
